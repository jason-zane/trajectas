# Trajectas Numerical Reasoning Assessment — Design Blueprint

**Status: DRAFT DESIGN. Not validated. Not for operational use.** See §12, Empirical validation requirements.

**Audience:** graduate-to-executive hiring populations (client-facing selection and shortlisting).
**Document purpose:** the reference blueprint the build follows — construct definition, blueprint, item-writing rules, and 12 fully documented sample items with exact stimulus data specifications.
**Language:** UK English throughout, including all candidate-facing text.

---

## 1. Construct definition

### 1.1 What the test measures

**Numerical reasoning** is here defined as: *the ability to draw correct conclusions from quantitative information presented in tables, charts and short verbal descriptions — by selecting the relevant figures, choosing the appropriate operation (proportion, rate, ratio, percentage change, weighted comparison), executing a short chain of such operations, and judging the plausibility of the result.*

The construct is **reasoning with numbers, not facility at producing them**. The intelligent work in every item is deciding *what to compute and from which figures* — identifying the correct base for a percentage, the correct direction of a ratio, the correct column of a table, the correct model for a rate problem. The arithmetic itself is deliberately trivial once the reasoning is done, and a calculator is provided (§4) precisely so that computation speed and mental-arithmetic fluency contribute as little variance as possible.

### 1.2 Place in the CHC hierarchy

In Cattell–Horn–Carroll terms the target is **Quantitative Reasoning (RQ)**, a narrow ability under **Fluid Reasoning (Gf)**: inductive and deductive reasoning where the relations to be manipulated are quantitative. The test necessarily draws on a thin layer of **Quantitative Knowledge (Gq)** — the meaning of percentages, ratios, rates, averages and simple index numbers — but this layer is capped at what any graduate-calibre candidate in any discipline can be assumed to hold (roughly UK GCSE Foundation content, in place by age 14). Nothing beyond that layer is required by any item, so Gq functions as a floor assumption, not a source of discriminating variance.

The test is designed to **minimise** loading on:

- **Gs (processing speed):** power-with-limit timing (§6), generous per-item time, calculator provided.
- **Grw (reading and writing):** hard caps on stem length and sentence complexity (§7.1).
- **Gsm (short-term memory):** the stimulus remains on screen at all times; no item requires holding intermediate values in memory (the on-screen calculator retains the running value).

### 1.3 What it predicts

General cognitive ability is the single best-evidenced psychometric predictor of job performance and training success, and quantitative reasoning is among its strongest indicators for roles involving analysis, budgeting, forecasting, commercial decision-making and data-informed judgement — i.e. most of the graduate-to-executive roles Trajectas clients hire for. The working prediction claim for this instrument is therefore: *scores should predict performance in roles where interpreting quantitative management information is part of the work.* This is a design hypothesis. It remains a hypothesis until the criterion studies in §12 are run, and no client-facing material may state or imply otherwise.

### 1.4 Construct boundaries — what this test must NOT measure

| Excluded source of variance | Why excluded | Design control |
|---|---|---|
| **Arithmetic speed / mental calculation** | Gs and drilled fluency, not reasoning; trainable; adverse to older candidates | On-screen calculator; power-with-limit timing; numbers chosen so no computation exceeds two calculator steps |
| **Mathematics curriculum knowledge** (algebra beyond linear one-unknown, geometry, trigonometry, statistics beyond mean, formal probability) | That is Gq/achievement, correlated with educational opportunity and field of study | Content audit rule: every item solvable with the four operations, percentages, ratios and the arithmetic mean |
| **Reading speed and verbal comprehension** | Grw contamination; penalises dyslexic and ESL candidates on a numerical construct | Reading-load rules in §7.1; automated readability gate on every stem |
| **Cultural, national or domain-specific knowledge** (sports, local tax rules, imperial units, financial-market conventions) | Construct-irrelevant and a DIF generator | Cultural-neutrality rules in §7.2 |
| **Business jargon familiarity** | Advantages candidates from particular functions/sectors | Permitted business vocabulary is a closed list (§7.1) |
| **Test-wiseness** | Rewards coaching, not ability | Option-writing rules in §8 |
| **Device and dexterity effects** | Construct-irrelevant | Mobile-first stimulus constraints in §7.3 |

### 1.5 Response process claim

The intended response process for every item is: (1) read the question; (2) locate the relevant figures in the stimulus; (3) select the operation(s); (4) execute (with calculator if wanted); (5) match to an option, or estimate and eliminate. Any item that can be answered reliably by a materially different process — pattern-matching on options, keyword spotting, prior domain knowledge — fails review regardless of its statistics. Cognitive-lab think-alouds during piloting (§12) verify this claim.

## 2. Measurement model and item format

- **Format:** single-best-answer multiple choice, 4 options standard, 5 options permitted where a fifth error path is genuinely plausible (never padded).
- **Scoring:** number correct (rights scoring). No formula/guessing correction — it adds construct-irrelevant risk-attitude variance and confuses candidates. Omissions score zero; candidates are told to answer every item.
- **Item independence:** items sharing a stimulus (testlets) are permitted — they mirror real data-interpretation work and amortise reading load — but no item may use another item's answer, and local dependence is checked at pilot (Yen's Q3; testlet model if flagged).
- **Delivery:** item-banked, fixed-form initially; the blueprint (§5) is written per form so parallel forms and, later, LOFT/CAT assembly inherit the same specification.

## 3. Item difficulty bands

Predicted difficulty is stated per item as a classical *p*-value band (proportion correct in the graduate-to-executive applicant population). Predictions are design targets to be replaced by pilot estimates.

| Band | Predicted p | Design intent |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | .75–.90 | Single extract or one-step computation; warm-up and floor coverage |
| Moderate | .55–.75 | Two-step computation or a comparison requiring the right base |
| Hard | .35–.55 | Multi-step chains, competing plausible bases, ratio direction traps |
| Very hard | .20–.35 | Inference combining two data dimensions or two stimuli quantities; ceiling coverage for executive-level discrimination |

Target form shape: information maximised around the shortlisting decision zone (roughly 60th–85th percentile of the applicant pool), so the operational form skews moderate–hard rather than symmetric.

## 4. Calculator policy

**Policy: a basic on-screen four-function calculator (with %, square root and one memory register) is provided inside the test player. Personal calculators, phones and spreadsheets are prohibited and the instructions say so.**

Justification:

1. **Construct alignment.** The construct is choosing the computation, not performing it. Removing the arithmetic burden increases the proportion of score variance attributable to reasoning. This is the settled industry norm for occupational numerical reasoning (SHL, Talent Q/Korn Ferry, Cubiks, cut-e all permit or provide a calculator), and departing from it would also make Trajectas scores non-comparable with candidate expectations.
2. **Fairness.** Mental-arithmetic fluency is heavily practice- and curriculum-dependent and declines with age; letting it drive scores invites age and educational DIF unrelated to the construct.
3. **Standardisation.** Providing the calculator *in the player* (rather than "bring your own") equates the tool across candidates and devices, and lets the platform log calculator usage as process data.
4. **Item-writing consequence.** Because the calculator exists, items must never be solvable by calculator grind alone: the discriminating step must always be figure selection, base selection, operation selection or model construction. Numbers are chosen so that the *wrong* reasoning also produces a clean number that appears among the distractors — the calculator will happily compute the wrong thing.

## 5. Test blueprint

### 5.1 Content areas

| Content area | Share of operational form (20 items) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tables** | 35% (7 items) | 2 shared-stimulus testlets + singletons; the workhorse format, closest to real management information |
| **Charts** (bar, line, pie/composition, indexed series) | 35% (7 items) | Max 2 series per chart (§7.3); every chart type must also pass the accessibility rule in §7.4 |
| **Word problems** (rates, mixtures/ratios, successive percentages) | 20% (4 items) | Stems bound by reading rules in §7.1; these carry the "model construction" operation |
| **Quantitative estimation** | 10% (2 items) | Options spaced ≥ ~8% apart; rewards approximation and plausibility judgement |
| **Number series** | **0% — excluded** | See §5.3 |

### 5.2 Cognitive operations

Every item is tagged with its dominant operation; each operation must appear in every form.

| Operation | Definition | Form share |
|---|---|---|
| **Extract** | Locate and read off the correct value(s) | 10–15% |
| **Compare** | Order or contrast values, possibly after normalising | 15–20% |
| **Compute** | Execute a determinate one/two-step calculation on selected figures | 30–35% |
| **Infer** | Chain operations or combine data dimensions to reach a conclusion not directly displayed | 25–30% |
| **Estimate** | Approximate under deliberate imprecision; judge plausibility | 10% |

Blueprint matrix (operational 20-item form; the 12 samples in §10 tile this matrix):

| | Extract | Compare | Compute | Infer | Estimate | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tables | 1 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 0 | 7 |
| Charts | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 7 |
| Word problems | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 4 |
| Estimation stimuli | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| **Total** | 2 | 3 | 7 | 6 | 2 | 20 |

### 5.3 Number series: considered and excluded

Number series (e.g. 3, 6, 12, 24, ?) were considered and are **excluded** from the operational blueprint, for four reasons:

1. **Construct misfit.** Series completion loads on Gf-Induction generally, not RQ specifically; the "quantitative" surface is incidental. It measures rule discovery over integer sequences, which is not the workplace construct clients are buying.
2. **Coachability.** Series items are the most coachable numerical format in existence: a small taxonomy of generating rules (constant difference, constant ratio, alternating, second-order differences, interleaved series) covers nearly the whole item universe, and commercial prep sites teach exactly that taxonomy. Coached practice effects of the resulting size are score contamination, and they are socioeconomically patterned — candidates who can pay for prep gain most.
3. **No ecological validity.** No executive ever completes a number series at work; tables, charts and rate problems mirror real management information. This matters for candidate reactions and legal defensibility (job-relatedness).
4. **Keyability risk.** Short series frequently admit multiple defensible continuations, producing arbitration disputes.

If a future client insists, series items may run as **unscored experimental content only**, and the exclusion rationale above goes in the technical manual.

## 6. Time-pressure policy

**Policy: power-with-limit.** The operational 20-item form has a **25-minute whole-test limit** (75 s/item average); individual items carry *guideline* times (shown in §10) used for form assembly, not enforced per item. There is no per-item countdown.

Rationale:

- **Pure power (untimed)** is rejected: in unproctored online delivery, unlimited time invites collaboration and lookup, degrades the standardisation claim, and inflates testing-fatigue variance.
- **Speeded** is rejected: speeding injects Gs variance into an RQ measure (construct contamination), penalises candidates with disabilities and ESL candidates disproportionately, and pushes candidates toward test-wise skipping strategies rather than reasoning.
- **Power-with-limit** keeps sessions schedulable and comparable while letting nearly all candidates attempt everything. **Calibration rule:** the limit is set at pilot so that ≥ 90% of the pilot sample reaches the final item and ≥ 95% attempt at least 18 of 20 items; 25 minutes is the design prior, and the pilot number wins. Not-reached rates are monitored operationally as a speededness index.
- **Extended-time accommodations** (typically +25% and +50% variants) are pre-built into the player, not improvised per request.

Per-item guideline times by band: Easy 45–60 s; Moderate 60–90 s; Hard 90–120 s; Very hard 120–150 s. The 12 samples below sum to ~19 minutes of guideline time, consistent with a 25-minute 20-item form once the band mix is applied.

## 7. Construct-irrelevant variance controls

### 7.1 Reading-load discipline

Numerical items must not be disguised verbal items. Hard rules, enforced by checklist and an automated readability gate before any item enters review:

1. **Stem length:** maximum 2 context sentences plus 1 question sentence. Word-problem stems: maximum 55 words total. Table/chart stems: maximum 35 words (the stimulus carries the information, not the prose).
2. **Sentence complexity:** maximum 20 words per sentence; maximum one subordinate clause per sentence; no nested conditionals ("if X, then unless Y…" is banned).
3. **Voice and tense:** active voice; simple present or simple past only.
4. **Negation:** no negative stems ("Which region did NOT…") and no negative options; double negatives banned outright.
5. **Vocabulary:** general vocabulary restricted to high-frequency English (target: CEFR B1–B2; readability gate at UK reading age ≤ 13 for all non-technical prose). Permitted quantitative/business terms form a closed list: *revenue, cost, budget, profit, margin, output, price, discount, rate, average, total, ratio, percentage, index, headcount, quarter (Q1–Q4)*. Additions require a sign-off, not an item writer's whim.
6. **No idiom, no humour, no scene-setting.** Context is one clause of framing ("A factory runs two production lines."), never a narrative.
7. **Numbers as digits**, never words; units stated once and consistently; thousands separators used from 1,000 upward; currency symbol before the figure; one currency per stimulus.
8. **The question sentence always ends the stem** and always contains the target quantity and its unit ("…what was the total Q2 revenue, in £?").

### 7.2 Cultural-neutrality rules

- Contexts are generic and organisation-internal: factories, regions, budgets, funds, production lines. No sports, religion, national holidays, food, celebrities, or country-specific institutions (tax bands, school grades, sports leagues).
- Region and entity names are semantically empty (North/South/East/West, Line A/Line B, Division Alpha/Beta) — no real place or company names, which carry unequal familiarity and drift out of date.
- Metric units only. Currency is a stated symbol with no exchange-rate knowledge ever required. No date-format ambiguity (months written as words; no DD/MM vs MM/DD exposure).
- No item depends on knowing how any real market, instrument or industry behaves; every needed fact is in the stimulus.
- Sensitivity review (trained reviewers, structured checklist) precedes piloting; DIF screening (§12) is the empirical backstop, not a substitute.

### 7.3 Device and mobile constraints

Items must be fully workable on a 360 px-wide phone screen without horizontal scrolling of the stimulus:

- **Tables:** maximum 5 columns × 6 data rows; column headers ≤ 2 lines; numbers right-aligned.
- **Charts:** maximum 2 data series and 10 categories/points; direct labelling preferred over legends; minimum rendered font equivalent to 12 px at 360 px width.
- **Layout:** stimulus above stem above stacked options; the stimulus remains reachable (sticky or one-flick scroll) while options are visible; nothing hides behind hover states (no hover on touch devices).
- **Interaction:** tap targets ≥ 44 px; single-tap answer selection with explicit confirm; the on-screen calculator opens as an overlay that never obscures the options.
- **Parity rule:** any item whose pilot statistics differ materially by device class (flagged via device-type DIF analysis) is repaired or retired; device is recorded for every response.

### 7.4 Accessibility

- **Colour:** colour is never the sole encoding. Chart series are distinguished by colour *plus* marker shape/line style *plus* direct labels; palettes are colour-blind-safe (Okabe–Ito). Pie/composition charts carry percentage labels on every segment, so the item is solvable from labels alone.
- **Contrast and zoom:** WCAG 2.2 AA contrast; layout survives 200% zoom and user font scaling.
- **Screen readers — the honest position.** Data tables are rendered as true HTML tables with header associations and are fully screen-reader accessible. Chart items are backed by a machine-readable data table (the exact specs in §10) exposed as the accessible alternative. For *extract/compute/infer* chart items this preserves the construct, because the reasoning does not depend on visual gestalt. But it must be stated plainly: an item whose intended work is *visual* comparison of magnitudes (e.g. Item 3's month-by-month comparison) is a different, easier-or-harder task when consumed as a table — the equivalence cannot be assumed, only asserted and then checked. The technical manual must say so, and accommodation-condition response data must be analysed separately rather than silently pooled. Where a client population includes screen-reader users, the table-first variants are the primary form for those candidates, and scores are interpreted with that caveat until comparability evidence exists.
- **Timing accommodations:** see §6.

## 8. Anti-test-wiseness rules

1. **Numeric options are always listed in ascending order.** This removes position-based cues entirely for numeric items and is disclosed in the candidate instructions.
2. **Key position balance is engineered at form level.** Because ascending order fixes each key's position mechanically, balance is achieved by designing distractor sets with error paths both above and below the key. The 12 samples below land at A×3, B×3, C×3, D×3 (the one 5-option item keys at D). Assembly software enforces: no key position > 35% of a form; no run of ≥ 3 identical key positions.
3. **No "all of the above", "none of the above", or "cannot say" options** in this instrument. ("Cannot say" belongs to verbal-critical-reasoning designs; here it only rewards ambiguity-hunting.)
4. **Homogeneous options:** identical units, identical formatting, identical decimal precision within an item. No option is conspicuously longer, more precise, or more hedged than the others.
5. **Every distractor is a documented error path** (wrong base, ratio inversion, unit slip, wrong column/row, premature rounding, additive-instead-of-multiplicative, ignored weighting). No filler numbers, no near-duplicates of the key differing only in the last digit (except where the error path genuinely produces that — and then spacing is checked at pilot via distractor analysis).
6. **No giveaway convergence:** the key must not be the only option that is "middle of the range", the only round number, or the only value sharing digits with the stimulus. Item writers check the item *as a test-wise candidate would*: if elimination logic alone finds the key, the item fails review.
7. **Estimation items space options ≥ 8% apart** so approximation is rewarded but option-set triangulation is not.

### Stimulus A — Regional sales table

Render as a data table, title: **"Product sales by region"**. Footnote under table: *"Revenue is shown in £000 (thousands of pounds)."*

| Region | Q1 units sold | Q1 revenue (£000) | Q2 units sold | Q2 revenue (£000) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North | 1,200 | 660 | 1,380 | 780 |
| South | 950 | 570 | 1,180 | 627 |
| East | 800 | 512 | 640 | 400 |
| West | 1,050 | 588 | 1,260 | 700 |

---

#### Item NR-01 — Tables / Extract–Compute · Easy

**Stem:** What was the total revenue across all four regions in Q2?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. £2,507
- B. £2,107,000
- C. £2,330,000
- D. **£2,507,000** ← key
- E. £4,460,000

**Worked solution:** Q2 revenue column: 780 + 627 + 400 + 700 = 2,507 (£000) = £2,507,000.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A £2,507 — **unit error**: summed the column correctly but ignored the £000 footnote.
- B £2,107,000 — **omitted row**: summed North, South and West only (780 + 627 + 700 = 2,107), skipping East.
- C £2,330,000 — **right operation, wrong column**: summed Q1 revenue (660 + 570 + 512 + 588 = 2,330).
- E £4,460,000 — **wrong measure**: summed Q2 *units* (1,380 + 1,180 + 640 + 1,260 = 4,460) and read the result as pounds.

**Predicted difficulty:** Easy, p ≈ .85. **Guideline time:** 50 s.
*(Five options used: all four error paths are independently plausible and empirically common.)*

---

#### Item NR-02 — Tables / Compare–Compute · Hard

**Stem:** Which region had the largest percentage increase in revenue from Q1 to Q2?

**Options (table order):**
- A. North
- B. South
- C. East
- D. **West** ← key

**Worked solution:** North: 120/660 = +18.2%. South: 57/570 = +10.0%. East: −112/512 = −21.9% (a decrease). West: 112/588 = +19.0%. Largest increase: West.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A North — **absolute-for-relative confusion**: North has the largest absolute increase (+£120k vs West's +£112k); candidates who never normalise choose North. This is the strong distractor and drives the item's difficulty.
- B South — **wrong column**: South has the largest percentage increase in *units* (950 → 1,180 = +24.2%); confusing units with revenue selects South.
- C East — **direction/sign error**: East shows the largest percentage *change* in magnitude (−21.9%); misreading "increase" as "change" selects East.

**Predicted difficulty:** Hard, p ≈ .50 (North–West gap is only 0.9 points, forcing genuine computation of all four). **Guideline time:** 110 s.

#### Item NR-03 — Charts / Compare · Easy

**Stem:** In how many months was Line B's output higher than Line A's?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. **1** ← key
- B. 2
- C. 3
- D. 5

**Worked solution:** Month-by-month: Jan 38 < 42; Feb 41 < 45; Mar 47 < 48; Apr 49 > 44 ✓; May 46 < 50; Jun 51 < 53. Only April. Answer: 1.

**Distractor rationales:**
- B 2 — **near-value misread**: counts March (47 vs 48, one unit apart on the chart) as a B win alongside April.
- C 3 — **level/change confusion**: counts the months in which Line B's *month-on-month growth* exceeded Line A's (Mar, Apr, Jun), answering a different question about slopes rather than levels.
- D 5 — **inverted comparison**: counts the months in which Line *A* exceeded Line B.

**Predicted difficulty:** Easy, p ≈ .80. **Guideline time:** 55 s.

#### Item NR-04 — Charts / Compute · Moderate

**Stem:** Line A's output in June was what percentage higher than its output in January?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. 11.0%
- B. 17.8%
- C. 20.8%
- D. **26.2%** ← key

**Worked solution:** (53 − 42) / 42 = 11/42 = 0.2619 → 26.2%.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A 11.0% — **difference read as percentage**: the raw gap (53 − 42 = 11 thousand units) reported as if it were the percentage.
- B 17.8% — **right operation, wrong data point**: uses February (45) as the base — (53 − 45)/45 = 17.8% — an adjacent-point misread on the chart.
- C 20.8% — **wrong base**: divides the increase by the *final* value — 11/53 = 20.8% — instead of the starting value.

**Predicted difficulty:** Moderate, p ≈ .68. **Guideline time:** 70 s.

#### Item NR-05 — Charts / Compute · Moderate

**Stem:** One third of the Technology budget is moved to Marketing. Marketing then holds what percentage of the total budget?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. 16%
- B. **17%** ← key
- C. 20%
- D. 22%

**Worked solution:** One third of Technology's 15 points = 5 points. Marketing: 12 + 5 = 17%.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A 16% — **fraction applied to the wrong base**: takes one third of *Marketing's* 12 points (4) and adds it — 12 + 4 = 16.
- C 20% — **right number, wrong category**: adds the 5 transferred points to *Technology's* 15 instead of Marketing's 12 (reads off the wrong segment after computing correctly).
- D 22% — **wrong portion transferred**: moves the remaining *two thirds* of Technology (10 points) — 12 + 10 = 22 — misreading which part is transferred.

**Predicted difficulty:** Moderate, p ≈ .65. **Guideline time:** 70 s.

---

#### Item NR-06 — Charts / Infer · Hard

**Stem:** Next year the total budget grows by 10% and the Salaries budget grows by 15%. Salaries will then be what percentage of the new total?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. 45.0%
- B. **47.0%** ← key
- C. 50.0%
- D. 51.8%

**Worked solution:** Salaries now: 0.45 × £3.6m = £1.62m. Next year: 1.62 × 1.15 = £1.863m. New total: 3.6 × 1.10 = £3.96m. Share: 1.863 / 3.96 = 0.4705 → 47.0%. (Shortcut: 45% × 1.15/1.10 = 47.0% — both routes accepted in the solution key.)

**Distractor rationales:**
- A 45.0% — **share assumed invariant**: fails to see that differential growth changes the mix.
- C 50.0% — **additive-instead-of-multiplicative**: adds the growth-rate difference to the share — 45 + (15 − 10) = 50.
- D 51.8% — **growth applied to the percentage itself**: 45% × 1.15 = 51.75%, growing the share by the salary growth rate and ignoring the total's growth.

**Predicted difficulty:** Hard, p ≈ .50. **Guideline time:** 105 s.

#### Item NR-07 — Word problems / Compute (rates) · Moderate

**Stem:** Machine P fills 240 bottles per hour. Machine Q fills 180 bottles per hour. Working together at these rates, how long do they take to fill 1,470 bottles?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. **3.5 hours** ← key
- B. 6.1 hours
- C. 7.0 hours
- D. 8.2 hours

**Worked solution:** Combined rate 240 + 180 = 420 bottles/hour. Time = 1,470 / 420 = 3.5 hours.

**Distractor rationales:**
- B 6.1 hours — **ignored the second machine**: 1,470 / 240 = 6.125 (Machine P alone).
- C 7.0 hours — **averaged instead of summed the rates**: 1,470 / 210 = 7.0, treating "together" as the mean rate.
- D 8.2 hours — **wrong single machine**: 1,470 / 180 = 8.17 (Machine Q alone).

**Predicted difficulty:** Moderate, p ≈ .70. **Guideline time:** 65 s.

---

#### Item NR-08 — Word problems / Infer (ratio) · Hard

**Stem:** A fund holds equities and bonds in the ratio 7 : 3. The fund sells £480,000 of equities and buys nothing. The ratio becomes 5 : 3. What was the fund's total value before the sale?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. £1,680,000
- B. £1,920,000
- C. **£2,400,000** ← key
- D. £3,840,000

**Worked solution:** Bonds are unchanged, so the "3" parts are the same size before and after. The equity side falls from 7 parts to 5 parts, so 2 parts = £480,000 and 1 part = £240,000. Initial total = 10 parts = £2,400,000. (Check: equities 7 × 240 = £1,680k → after sale £1,200k; 1,200 : 720 = 5 : 3 ✓.)

**Distractor rationales:**
- A £1,680,000 — **right process, wrong quantity reported**: this is the initial *equities* value (7 parts), not the total.
- B £1,920,000 — **post-sale total mistaken for pre-sale**: 2,400 − 480 = 1,920; answers the wrong time point.
- D £3,840,000 — **wrong part size**: treats £480,000 as *one* part and multiplies by the 8 post-sale parts (480 × 8).

**Predicted difficulty:** Hard, p ≈ .45. **Guideline time:** 110 s.

#### Item NR-09 — Word problems / Compute (successive percentages) · Moderate

**Stem:** A supplier raises all prices by 8%. It then offers a 12% discount on the new prices. Compared with the original prices, what is the net change?

**Options (ascending by net effect):**
- A. **5.0% lower** ← key
- B. 4.0% lower
- C. 3.0% higher
- D. 5.0% higher

**Worked solution:** 1.08 × 0.88 = 0.9504 → 4.96% ≈ 5.0% lower than original.

**Distractor rationales:**
- B 4.0% lower — **additive error**: −12 + 8 = −4, adding percentages instead of compounding them.
- C 3.0% higher — **operators swapped**: applies 8% as the discount and 12% as the rise — 1.12 × 0.92 = 1.0304 → +3.0%.
- D 5.0% higher — **direction/sign error**: correct magnitude, wrong direction (concludes the rise dominates).

**Predicted difficulty:** Moderate, p ≈ .60. **Guideline time:** 75 s.

#### Item NR-10 — Estimation / Estimate · Hard

**Stem:** A country's exports were $47.3 billion in 2020. Exports grew by about 8% each year. Approximately what were exports in 2025?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. $51 billion
- B. $66 billion
- C. **$70 billion** ← key
- D. $85 billion

**Worked solution:** Five years of compound growth: 1.08⁵ ≈ 1.469. 47.3 × 1.469 ≈ $69.5bn → approximately $70bn. An estimator's route: 8% for 5 years is 40% simple, plus compounding lift ≈ 47% total; 47.3 × 1.47 ≈ 70.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A $51 billion — **single-period error**: applies one year's growth only (47.3 × 1.08 ≈ 51.1).
- B $66 billion — **ignored compounding**: simple interest — 47.3 × 1.40 = 66.2.
- D $85 billion — **decimal slip on the rate**: treats five years at 8% as growth of 0.8 (47.3 × 1.8 ≈ 85).

**Predicted difficulty:** Hard, p ≈ .50. **Guideline time:** 90 s. *(Option spacing: adjacent options differ by ≥ 6%; key-to-nearest-distractor gap 6%, at the floor of the §8 rule and flagged for distractor-spacing review at pilot.)*

#### Item NR-11 — Tables / Infer · Very hard

**Stimulus (data table), title "Division performance, last year":**

| Division | Headcount | Revenue (€m) | Operating margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alpha | 320 | 48.0 | 15% |
| Beta | 400 | 60.0 | 24% |

**Stem:** Operating profit equals revenue multiplied by operating margin. Beta's operating profit per employee is what percentage higher than Alpha's?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. 0%
- B. 37.5%
- C. **60%** ← key
- D. 100%

**Worked solution:** Alpha profit: 48.0 × 0.15 = €7.2m → 7,200,000/320 = €22,500 per employee. Beta profit: 60.0 × 0.24 = €14.4m → 14,400,000/400 = €36,000 per employee. Excess: (36,000 − 22,500)/22,500 = 0.60 → 60%.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A 0% — **wrong numerator**: uses *revenue* per employee, which is €150,000 for both divisions — the stimulus is engineered so this error yields exact equality and a confident wrong answer.
- B 37.5% — **wrong base**: divides the €13,500 gap by Beta's €36,000 instead of Alpha's €22,500.
- D 100% — **ignored the denominator dimension**: compares total profits (14.4/7.2 = 2.0 → "100% higher") without adjusting for headcount.

**Predicted difficulty:** Very hard, p ≈ .35. **Guideline time:** 130 s.

#### Item NR-12 — Charts / Infer–Estimate · Very hard

**Stimulus:** **two-series indexed line chart**, title: **"House prices and average earnings, indexed (2015 = 100)"**. Both series labelled directly at the right edge; values labelled at 2015, 2020 and 2024 only (interior points unlabelled). Exact series (also the accessible data table):

| Year | House price index | Earnings index |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2015 | 100 | 100 |
| 2016 | 104 | 102 |
| 2017 | 109 | 104 |
| 2018 | 113 | 106 |
| 2019 | 118 | 108 |
| 2020 | 121 | 109 |
| 2021 | 124 | 110 |
| 2022 | 126 | 112 |
| 2023 | 128 | 113 |
| 2024 | 130 | 115 |

**Stem:** Between 2015 and 2024, house prices *relative to earnings* rose by approximately what percentage?

**Options (ascending):**
- A. 11.5%
- B. **13%** ← key
- C. 15%
- D. 30%

**Worked solution:** Both indices start at 100, so the ratio of 2024 indices gives the relative change: 130 / 115 = 1.1304 → ≈ 13%.

**Distractor rationales:**
- A 11.5% — **ratio inversion**: computes 115/130 = 0.885 and reports the 11.5% shortfall as the answer (the inverted ratio's complement).
- C 15% — **index points read as the answer**: takes the 2024 gap in index points (130 − 115 = 15) as a percentage — the classic index-number error.
- D 30% — **ignored the comparator**: reports house-price growth alone (100 → 130), dropping "relative to earnings" from the computation.

**Predicted difficulty:** Very hard, p ≈ .30 (index-relative reasoning is the least-practised operation in the blueprint). **Guideline time:** 130 s.

## 11. Form assembly and operational notes

- Operational form: 20 items per the §5 matrix; the 12 samples seed the bank, with each blueprint cell needing ≥ 4 bank items before parallel forms can be assembled.
- Item order: roughly easy → hard, with the two easiest items first (anxiety management and instructions check), and no two very-hard items adjacent.
- Two unscored pilot items may ride in each operational form (positions 8–15, never first or last), disclosed generically in candidate instructions.
- Practice: two fully worked example items (one table, one chart) precede the timed section and are excluded from scoring and the time limit.
- Integrity: item and option order fixed within a form (ascending rule); form-level randomisation across candidates; response-time and calculator-usage logs retained for verification testing and aberrance screening.

## 12. Empirical validation requirements (mandatory)

**These are draft blueprints. Nothing in this document is validated, and none of the predicted difficulties, timing parameters or predictive claims may be presented to clients or candidates as established properties.** Before operational use, all of the following are required:

1. **Content validation.** Independent SME review (≥ 3 reviewers: two psychometricians, one hiring-domain expert) rating every item for blueprint fit, single-key defensibility and construct relevance; structured sensitivity/fairness review per §7.2. Items failing either review are repaired and re-reviewed or dropped.
2. **Cognitive laboratories.** Think-aloud protocols with 10–15 target-population candidates to verify the §1.5 response-process claim, catch ambiguity, and confirm the calculator is used as intended.
3. **Pilot calibration.** Administer to a motivated sample representative of the applicant population: **N ≥ 400 per form** for stable classical statistics (p, item-total correlations, distractor endorsement, option-level functioning); **N ≥ 1,000 pooled** if 2PL/3PL IRT calibration is to anchor the bank. Retain/repair rules set in advance (e.g. item-total r ≥ .20; every distractor endorsed by ≥ 3% with negative point-biserial; no distractor endorsement exceeding the key's among high scorers). Testlet items checked for local dependence (Yen's Q3).
4. **Timing calibration.** Empirical verification of the §6 rule (≥ 90% reaching the last item); adjust the whole-test limit before fixing norms. Not-reached and response-time distributions reported by demographic group to confirm the test functions as power, not speed.
5. **Reliability.** Internal consistency (alpha/omega ≥ .80 target for a 20-item form; report with confidence intervals) and, for any retest or verification use, alternate-form or test-retest coefficients from a dedicated study (N ≥ 150, 2–6 week interval).
6. **DIF screening.** Mantel–Haenszel and logistic-regression DIF for sex, ethnicity, age band, ESL status and device class, with **≥ 200 per focal group** as the working minimum; B/C-flagged items repaired or removed and forms re-balanced. Repeat after any item repair.
7. **Criterion validation.** Concurrent and/or predictive studies against job performance, training success or work-sample criteria in client populations: **N ≥ 150 per study** as a floor, with meta-analytic pooling across clients as the programme matures. Adverse-impact monitoring (selection-ratio analysis) runs alongside, not after.
8. **Construct evidence.** Convergent correlations with an established numerical reasoning measure and discriminant evidence against verbal reasoning and Gs markers, to substantiate the §1 claims.
9. **Norming.** Occupation-level norm groups (graduate, professional/managerial, executive) of **N ≥ 500 each**, refreshed on a stated cycle; no score report ships against a norm group smaller than that.
10. **Accessibility comparability.** Separate analysis of accommodation conditions (extended time, screen-reader/table-first variants) per §7.4 before any claim of score comparability is made.
11. **Documentation.** A technical manual recording all of the above, the §5.3 number-series exclusion rationale, and the standing caveat that predictive claims are hypotheses until §12.7 evidence exists — maintained under version control alongside the item bank.

Until steps 1–6 are complete for a form, that form is **pilot material only** and must not contribute to any hiring decision.
